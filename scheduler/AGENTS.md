# scheduler/

- ESPHome compiles every C and C++ file in this folder into the firmware, so tests, tools and scratch code live elsewhere. scheduler_component.cpp is the component (the entities, the settings and the tick), requests.cpp the requests task (the downloads and the phone message) and settings_page.cpp the settings routes. Other files, like this one, are fine.
- charger.h, settings.h and the headers they include, calendar.h, market.h, savings.h, schedule.h and tariff.h, are built as plain C++17 by the unit tests, without ESPHome, and as gnu++20 in the firmware. Keep them C++17 and free of ESPHome headers. test/mutation_test.py lists the seven: a new header goes there too.
- The settings are a file on the board, which the page writes (a custom plan goes under `grid: plan:`, each line indented by four spaces), and which read_settings() in settings.h reads and checks before the board saves it, and again at every start: in the plans' YAML, with the line reading tariff.h's read_tariff() shares (next_line(), key_value()), and without quotes or comments after a value, by request. The settings routes, like ESPHome's own, answer only the board's own page: a request with another Origin gets 400, while curl, which sends none, works. Settings apply at once, and device.yaml's Bluetooth lambda hands a new VIN to the Tesla part; on a board that has a car, any change but the ntfy topic's or the unlock box's (`tesla_unlock_when_charged`) deletes the schedule, as Delete schedule does, by request (deletes_schedule() in settings.h), as neither changes it, while charging now stays; a board that has a car restarts only for another car, market area or currency, by request (restarts() in settings.h, which the settings route asks before it answers): the Bluetooth link and key are the car's, and the prices downloaded already are the area's, in its currency. The page's setup saves its first step's, the prices, as settings without the car's tesla_ lines, which read_settings() takes as no car yet, all three or none: the board then shows No car yet and does nothing until the setup's car step adds them. Without prices, the car charges as usual. A new setting needs its place in read_settings()'s table, its check, and its field in web/web_ui.js, as users have no other way to set it. A check made stricter turns away settings a board saved before: the page then asks for them again. Restart setup saves an empty settings file, which reads as none, puts the buttons' hold, the pairing and Ready by back to their defaults, presses the Tesla's Regenerate key, which saves the new key at once, and restarts the board: ESPHome's Wi-Fi and API key stay, and so do the savings, by request, as a currency change in Settings keeps them too and Reset savings clears them.
- Entity names are an interface. The page finds the board's entities by name (web/web_ui.js), this component finds the Tesla's by name, and Ready by and Ready by once are saved under keys ESPHome makes from their names. Renaming an entity, or an ESPHome release that makes those keys differently, silently loses the saved values: check after upgrading ESPHome.
- esphome-tesla-ble, at the pinned `tesla_ble_ref`: the car reports its security state (Asleep, User Present, the locks and the Charge Port Door) to any key, one it doesn't know too, so only a charging report (the Charger, Charging, Battery) shows the key is paired; the "Charger" binary sensor keeps its state once the car has reported it (only the asleep and user-present sensors go back to unknown), until the component clears it for a key the car turned away, as ESPHome calls no callback for the same state again; with the charging state "Unknown" it also reports the Charger as off, which isn't an unplug; the battery level and charge limit keep their last values; the charging states are "Disconnected", "No Power", "Starting", "Charging", "Complete", "Stopped", "Calibrating" and "Unknown". The controller relies on all of these. Its Charge Port Latch lock unlocks with Unlock Charge Port (CarServer's chargePortDoorOpen), a charging action, which releases a plugged-in cable: the component unlocks it on the tick the car goes from charging to Complete, where the settings ask for it (`tesla_unlock_when_charged`). Its Last Command text sensor, there though Home Assistant hides it by default, reports each of its commands' results, like `Wake → Failed: VCSEC key not on whitelist - pairing required` from a key the car doesn't know: the component then forgets the pairing, and the page brings back the setup's key step until the car reports again. Its polls report no errors, so a key removed in the car shows only when the board's own command fails, by the owner's choice over a change upstream. Pair BLE Key sends the request, which the car keeps until the key card, and then waits for an answer the car doesn't send: it sends the request again every 30 s, 6 times, and runs no other command for those 3 minutes (the log says "Max retries exceeded for command: Whitelist Add Key", and the commands queued behind it time out at once). A Bluetooth reconnect empties its queue, so the component turns the BLE Connection switch off and on 10 s after a pairing request. The plug state that shows the pairing comes with the next infotainment poll, every 30 s at first and every 11 minutes once the board has waited 11 minutes idle (`infotainment_sleep_timeout`), so the component also presses Force data update every 15 s for 3 minutes after a pairing request. Its binary sensors don't call on_state callbacks for their first state (`trigger_on_initial_state` is off), so the component hears the first plug state through add_full_state_callback.
- Savings goes to flash byte for byte under the key "scheduler_savings", and ESPHome loads it only into a struct of the same size, which a static_assert holds: another size starts every board's figures afresh, and another layout of the same size misreads them. To change it, read the old layout under its key and save the new one under another.
- Memory is tight. ESPHome's psram puts in PSRAM only what asks for it (`SPIRAM_USE_CAPS_ALLOC`), so a std::string or std::vector comes from the internal RAM that Wi-Fi, Bluetooth and HTTPS share, and one that can't grow restarts the board: an Abort in the crash report, from operator new. HTTPS takes over 20 kB of it while it runs (mbedTLS's buffers), and the update check, which the page starts when it opens, runs in a task of its own at the same time as the web server. So the web server's answers go out in chunks rather than in one big string, as /settings/options does, and a download too long to hold comes in through a filter as it arrives, as OKTE's 67 kB a day does (read_body_() with okte_filter()), and SEMOpx's 20 kB, kept to the area's prices (semopx_kept()).
- The loop mustn't wait on the network. ESPHome queues the Bluetooth events for it, 99 at most, which continuous scanning filled in under 2 s in the owner's garage, and then drops them, the car's too: a tick that downloaded both days' prices and the plan took 5 s after a restart and dropped 208. An HTTPS request takes the board a second or two, a new TLS connection every time, so the downloads and the phone message go out from a task of their own, one batch at a time, so the component holds one TLS session at a time, with its stack in PSRAM (psram's request_external_task_stack() in __init__.py), and hand each answer to the loop with defer(), as the loop uses the prices, the plan and the message meanwhile. A batch's end ticks at once, so new prices make the schedule as soon as they're in, as when the downloads ran in the tick. The task reads only the batch's copy of the settings (Requests): a save replaces the strings it would read.
- ESP-IDF's web server keeps 7 connections and, for another, closes the one whose last request is the oldest (ESPHome turns that on), which was a page's live stream: two or three pages open at once kept closing each other's streams and reconnecting. keep_streams() marks the streams as used at every request, from the settings page's canHandle(), which ESPHome calls for every request only while that handler comes before its own: load_settings() adds it, before any setup(). It finds the streams as the connections with a session context, which ESPHome's /events alone sets: check after upgrading ESPHome.
- Controller::observe_() runs before the schedule is made, so it judges a start from the car against the previous tick's schedule. On the first tick after a restart there's none, and in_schedule_() is true.
