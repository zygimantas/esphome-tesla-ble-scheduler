# test/

## Unit tests

- Build and run them from the repository root; the include paths assume it, and the tests read plans/.
- scheduler_test_tesla.h, the simulated Tesla, is also the simulation's car: keep it free of unit-test code.
- CHECK is a macro: a comma outside parentheses, as in `std::vector<std::pair<A, B>>`, splits its argument. Use a type alias.
- A plug-in counts as one (the phone message, a fresh schedule mode) only after a tick with the car unplugged: the first plug state the controller sees is taken as the state after a restart.
- On the first tick there's no schedule yet, so a start from the car on that tick doesn't switch to charging now, unless Stop charging was in force. Tick once before.
- simulate() ticks every 30 s and can't tell a 2-minute limit from a 1:58 one. Test limits with your own ticks just before and at the boundary.

## The simulation

- ESPHome 2026.10's precompiled header, forced into every file (`-include esphome_pch.h`), breaks the host build: logger_host.cpp's `time()` finds `namespace esphome::time` (posix_tz.h, with a time zone), and on a Mac httplib.h's MacTypes.h finds ArduinoJson's `Ptr`. `ESPHOME_PCH_ENABLE=0` leaves it out, in the workflows and CONTRIBUTING.md; the firmware builds with it. Try without it at the next ESPHome update.
- Its program needs a terminal and a time limit: `ESPHOME_PREFDIR=$(mktemp -d) python3 -c 'import pty, sys; pty.spawn(sys.argv[1:])' perl -e 'alarm 45; exec @ARGV' test/.esphome/build/simulation/.pioenvs/simulation/program > log`, then `grep -F '][scheduler' log`. ESPHOME_PREFDIR starts it without what an earlier run saved, like the buttons' mode, Ready by and savings.
- Without CHEAP_NOW=1, the outcome depends on the time of day, as the made-up prices are cheap from 01:00 to 05:00. A first "Wake the car (Reading battery)" is a startup race with the made-up sensors, not a bug, and the start may then wait for the next tick, 30 s later: give the program well over 30 s.

## The page

- fake_board.py serves the page as the board does, web/web_ui.js as /0.js and web/web_ui.css as /0.css under ESPHome's index, /events with a `ping` on connecting and every 10 s, /settings and /settings/options and the entities' REST routes, and models only what the page needs of the board. The status follows the settings: No settings yet, No car yet, Not paired until Pair BLE Key is pressed, then Unplugged, and with the car plugged in No schedule, Charging now or Charges at the next full hour, with one window, as the schedule buttons choose. A settings file goes through settings_tool, which is read_settings(), settings_options(), restarts() and deletes_schedule() on a computer (settings_tool.cpp, built like the unit tests to test/settings_tool, which git ignores), so the answers are the board's. Restart board, Restart setup, a save the board restarts for and Update's install restart it: every connection drops, nothing is answered for 2 s, and the uptime starts again. The update entity is UNKNOWN until Check for update, then UPDATE AVAILABLE with the release set through /fake, or NO UPDATE. What a request changes is published 0.2 s after its answer, as the board's next tick does: a state before the answer races the page. Not modelled: prices and the schedule itself, phone messages, the car's wake and a key the car turns away later.
- By hand: `python3 test/fake_board.py 8765 [settings-file]`, then open http://127.0.0.1:8765; port 8765 may be held by an older fake. A settings file is read at the start and written at each save. POST /fake with JSON (settings, paired, plugged, hold, update) starts the board afresh in that state, and GET /fake answers the state, with the POSTs received.
- page_test.py drives it in Google Chrome, headless, through Playwright's channel chrome, the Chrome installed on GitHub's runners and a Mac, so nothing is downloaded: `uvx --from "playwright==1.63.0" python test/page_test.py` from the repository root, with test/settings_tool built (PLAYWRIGHT_VERSION in ci.yml, pinned in CONTRIBUTING.md too). It starts the fake board on a free port and asserts on the page's text and on the POSTs the board received, without the page's own Check for update press as it opens. A JavaScript error on the page fails its scenario: that's how the setup's DOMException was found. Its VIN, 5YJ3E1EA6KF000000, passes the page's check digit; the unit tests' 5YJ3E1EA0KF000000 doesn't, as read_settings() doesn't check it.
- The Schedule scenario picks the first Ready by offered that isn't the board's, as the board's own isn't sent. A restart keeps the board away for about 3 s and the page reconnects 3 s after it asked, or when EventSource retries, so expect's timeout is 15 s. The run takes about a minute, most of it those waits.

## The README's savings

- savings_example.cpp works out the README's Example of yearly savings and its list of countries: every night of a year, from October 2025, when the market prices went to quarter-hours, to September 2026, for 20,000 km a year, on every plan in plans/ of the countries where homes pay market prices, else on the one regulated or fixed price (plan_text), at 2026's fees, taxes and margins all year. It compares the board's make_schedule() with charging at once from 18:00, the car's own schedule and the start that cost least over the year, found afterwards, each with the energy the board's schedule takes. The car's own schedule starts when the plan's cheapest fee of the night does on a Wednesday, in the month that's latest, or at midnight where the fee is the same all night (night_start()). CI only builds it, as it downloads the prices.
- Each country takes a dynamic contract, except where homes can't have one or it costs more than their usual price, as in Bulgaria, Croatia, Hungary, Ireland, Slovakia and Switzerland: there it takes that regulated or fixed price, as a custom plan without a market price (Source::NONE). Monthly fees and fees per kW are left out.
- The program prints the README's list of countries last, by name, each with a table of its plans, in the order of their files, and its confidence, and an icon before its name: 🟢 where a plan saves 15% or more against the car's own schedule and the confidence isn't low, ⚪ where nothing is saved, 🟡 else, so a rerun's list goes into the README as it is. It shows only the saving against the car's own schedule, which is what an owner has without the board. Confidence is high where many homes pay the prices the example takes, medium where a few percent do or most chose another price, and low where hardly any do or the bill follows other prices.
- Where a country has several market areas (Sweden, Norway, Denmark), each plan takes the one most of its company's homes are in, take_plan()'s table: Glitre Nett and Lede serve two areas each, E.ON Syd and Vattenfall more. take_plan() also leaves Vienna's levy to Wiener Netze, and VAT out of Arva's, as Norway's north pays none.
- Each country's decisions, figures, sources and confidence are in plans/<country>/AGENTS.md, researched in October 2026 by one agent per country: start there rather than researching afresh.
- Nord Pool serves only its last two months without an account, so the prices come from others that publish the same: Elering's dashboard for the Baltics and Finland, elprisetjustnu.se for Sweden, Energy-Charts for Norway and Romania, SMARD for the rest, all matching Nord Pool's in euros where checked, and other currencies at the ECB's daily rate within half a percent of Nord Pool's. OMIE, for Spain and Portugal, keeps only a day's latest file: 30 October 2025's is its third. Energy-Charts answers quick repeats with 429, so the program waits 10 s before each new file, and allows private use only: the downloads stay in test/.prices, which git ignores.
- Redo it after the plans' prices change in January (#161), for the last 12 months at the new prices, taxes, levies and margins, then each country's AGENTS.md, the README's list and the example above it, Sweden's Vattenfall Eldistribution Tidstariff row with the plan's name above the table, so its four figures fit on one line.

## Mutation testing

- A run takes about 40 minutes on 8 cores and builds scheduler_test.cpp for every mutant: don't edit it while a run is going. Survivor line numbers refer to the headers as they were when the run started. On the owner's Mac a run took 5 hours in October 2026, with XProtect checking every new program and ReportCrash reporting every mutant that crashes.
- Hundreds of survivors (about 240 before the COUNTRIES table) are entries of three tables, AREAS in market.h and COUNTRIES and TIME_ZONES in settings.h: data, which a test would only repeat.
- Around 280 others are equivalent; don't chase them: spare buffer sizes, the order of declarations, defaults overwritten before use, `a % b` standing in for a time difference `a - b`, swapped std::min and std::max arguments, `<=` for `==` on values that can't be negative or on an enum's first or last value, iterator `<` for `!=`, `>=` or `<` against an end that find never passes, a `continue;` at the end of a loop body or a `break;` after a `return`, sscanf's count against more than it converts, constants with slack in the calendar formulas or within a test's float tolerance, comparisons with `npos` or within a closed set of names (read_tariff()'s sections, the clock's two values), -2 for the -1 that marks a non-digit or `% '0'` for `- '0'` on a digit in two_digits(), battery levels with a fraction, as a Tesla reports whole percents, and input no market or the ECB sends, like a rate under 0.1, OMIE's header twice or a price off the quarter-hours.
- Look closely at any other survivor: one found a real bug, where a button erased the board's memory of its own start.
