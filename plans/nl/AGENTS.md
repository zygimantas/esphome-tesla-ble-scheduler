# plans/nl/

Research for the README's Example of yearly savings (test/savings_example.cpp), checked in October 2026: what a kWh costs here besides the market price. Monthly fees are left out, as they don't depend on when the car charges.

- **Dynamic contracts:** common and growing. 8% of households had one in August 2026, 55% a fixed price and 37% a variable one (ACM, 13 August 2026: https://www.acm.nl/nl/publicaties/energiemonitor-acm-toename-aantal-dynamische-contracten-zet-door). For electricity that was 573,000 consumers on 1 January 2026, 7% of households, and 651,000 on 1 June 2026 (the ACM's figures in Solar Magazine: https://solarmagazine.nl/nieuws-zonne-energie/i43038/573-000-nederlanders-hebben-dynamisch-energiecontract-marktaandeel-anwb-krimpt and https://solarmagazine.nl/nieuws-zonne-energie/i44318/zonneplan-wint-marktaandeel-aantal-dynamische-contracten-blijft-groeien). Their shares on 1 June 2026: ANWB Energie 25 - 30%, Zonneplan 20 - 25%, NextEnergy 10 - 15%, and Budget Energie, Frank Energie, Eneco and Tibber 5 - 10% each. All bill the Dutch day-ahead price (EPEX's, the same as Nord Pool's), set by the quarter-hour since 1 October 2025, plus the supplier's margin, the energy tax and VAT. Tibber (https://tibber.com/nl/stroomprijs) and Zonneplan (https://www.zonneplan.nl/energie/dynamische-energieprijzen/stroomprijs) bill each quarter-hour, and so do Coolblue Energie and Noord Energie (Overstappen.nl, 2025). Frank Energie lets the customer choose (https://www.frankenergie.nl/nl/dynamisch-energiecontract). ANWB Energie, NextEnergy and Essent bill each hour at the average of its four quarter-hours: NextEnergy's and Essent's pages say so (https://www.nextenergy.nl/actuele-energieprijzen, https://www.essent.nl/energie/energiecontract/dynamisch), and ANWB's hourly prices are that average to the cent, as its price API has no quarter-hours (https://api.anwb.nl/energy/energy-services/v2/tarieven/electricity?startDate=2026-09-14T22:00:00.000Z&endDate=2026-09-15T21:59:59.999Z&interval=HOUR). Eneco and Vattenfall price by the hour too. So about half the dynamic customers pay hourly averages.
- **VAT:** 21% on the whole bill, the energy tax and the margin included, in 2025 and 2026 (Rijksoverheid: https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/vraag-en-antwoord/energie-thuis/opbouw-energierekening). Frank Energie's price API adds 21% to the market price on all 56 days checked across the year.
- **Per kWh, the same every hour:** only the energy tax (energiebelasting): €0.09161 without VAT and €0.11085 with VAT in 2026, €0.10154 and €0.12286 in 2025. The rate is for the first 10,000 kWh a year (0 - 2,900 and 2,901 - 10,000 kWh pay the same), which a home with the car stays within. The Belastingdienst's table has the rates without VAT (https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/bldcontentnl/belastingdienst/zakelijk/overige_belastingen/belastingen_op_milieugrondslag/energiebelasting/energiebelasting). Rijksoverheid gives 11.1 cents with VAT for 2026, Zonneplan's page €0.11085 for 2026 and €0.12286 for 2025 (https://www.zonneplan.nl/energie/dynamische-energieprijzen/kwh-prijs), and Frank Energie's API bills €0.12286 until 31 December 2025 and €0.11085 from 1 January 2026. The ODE levy has been 0 since 2023, and the reduced rate for charging installations applies in neither year. Their sum with VAT: €0.11085. Left out, as fixed: the yearly tax reduction (vermindering energiebelasting), €519.80 without VAT and €629 with VAT in 2026 (€524.95 without VAT in 2025), the grid operator's yearly charges and the supplier's monthly fee. Nothing else changes with the hour: no holiday rules apply to dynamic prices or the grid. Net metering (salderingsregeling) nets a solar home's yearly feed-in against its use, energy tax included, until it ends on 1 January 2027 (https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/themas/klimaat-milieu-en-natuur/energie-thuis/salderingsregeling). It doesn't change which night hours are cheapest.
- **Supplier's margin:** €0.01900 with VAT (€0.01570 without), the middle of the five biggest offers, weighted by their share of dynamic customers. Each margin (inkoopvergoeding), read on 11 October 2026:
  - ANWB Energie: €0.01800 (€0.01488 without VAT), €8.50 a month (https://www.anwb.nl/energie/actuele-tarieven, https://www.anwb.nl/energie/vaste-leveringskosten-energie). Its hourly prices are the market price with VAT plus €0.11085 and €0.01800.
  - Zonneplan: €0.02000 (€0.01653), the handling fee in every price on its page (https://www.zonneplan.nl/energie/dynamische-energieprijzen/stroomprijs), €6.25 a month (Overstappen.nl, 5 October 2026, which gives €0.02200).
  - NextEnergy: €0.02100 (€0.01736), €5.99 a month (https://www.nextenergy.nl/dynamische-energie). Its comparison table says €0.02190, and a customer pays the margin of when they joined.
  - Frank Energie: €0.01815 (€0.01500) on all 56 days checked from 1 October 2025 to 30 September 2026 in its price API (https://graphql.frankenergie.nl, sourcingMarkupPrice), €6.00 a month (Gaslicht, 7 October 2026).
  - Tibber: €0.01800 (€0.01488), €6.99 a month (https://tibber.com/nl/stroomprijs). Its prices shown on 11 October 2026 were the market price with VAT plus €0.11085 and €0.01800.
  - In October - December 2025 ANWB's margin was about €0.02000, worked out from its hourly prices, and its monthly fee €7.00. Frank Energie's was the same as in 2026.
- **The grid:** no fee per kWh, so no plan. Grid operators charge a home a fixed amount a year by the size of its connection, which the supplier bills monthly. For 3x25A in 2026 Liander charges €478.04 with VAT a year (https://www.liander.nl/-/media/files/tarieven/consument/2026/jaarlijkse-netwerkkosten-stroom-2026-v10.pdf) and Stedin €476.76 (https://www.stedin.net/-/media/project/online/files/tarieven/tarieven-2026/tarieven-2026_elektriciteit_kleinverbruik.pdf), in fixed parts only: connection, transport, capacity and meter. Tibber's page agrees: its kWh price has the market price, margin, energy tax and VAT, not the grid charges. Without a plan there's no cheap fee to start the car's own schedule at. The off-peak (dal) hours of fixed and variable contracts with two rates are usually 23:00 - 07:00 on weekdays and all day at weekends and on public holidays, in some regions from 21:00 (https://www.liander.nl/tarieven/enkeltarief-of-dubbeltarief). The ACM planned to decide on grid fees by the time of day for households at the end of 2026, to start shortly after large users' in 2028 (Focus op Energie 2026: https://www.acm.nl/system/files/documents/focus-op-energie-2026_0.pdf).
- **Prices to download:** SMARD's chart data, series 256: the Netherlands' day-ahead prices in euros per MWh, by the quarter-hour from 1 October 2025, a week a file from Monday 00:00 German time, without an account or key. https://www.smard.de/app/chart_data/256/DE/256_DE_quarterhour_1759096800000.json is the week from Monday 29 September 2025, https://www.smard.de/app/chart_data/256/DE/index_quarterhour.json lists the weeks, and market.h's smard_url(256, ...) makes the URL. It has all 35,136 quarter-hours from 1 October 2025 to 1 October 2026. It's the price the contracts bill: it matched Nord Pool's NL prices, which the board downloads, on all 4,800 quarter-hours from 12 August to 30 September 2026, Energy-Charts' (https://api.energy-charts.info/price?bzn=NL&start=2025-10-01&end=2025-10-31) on every quarter-hour of October and December 2025, and the market prices Frank Energie bills on 53 days spread over the year, both clock changes included (5,088 quarter-hours). Tibber's, Zonneplan's and ANWB's prices follow from it too.
- **Uncertain:**
  - Who bills by the hour or the quarter-hour comes from the suppliers' pages in October 2026, and for Coolblue Energie, Noord Energie and Budget Energie from comparison sites in 2025.
  - The calculation bills each quarter-hour. On an hourly contract the saving is smaller: a rough check on these prices, with the cheapest quarter-hours before VAT rather than the board's make_schedule(), puts it about a tenth smaller, as the quarter-hours picked cost about 0.7 cents per kWh more at their hour's average.
  - ANWB's margin before 2026 is worked out from its prices, rounded to the cent, not stated anywhere; it changed around 1 January 2026.
  - The suppliers' shares are the ACM's ranges as Solar Magazine reports them; the ACM's own page gives only the total.
  - Zonneplan's and Frank Energie's monthly fees come from comparison sites.
  - Whether the grid operators' proposal for fees by the time of day reached the ACM, and when such fees start, wasn't checked further.
